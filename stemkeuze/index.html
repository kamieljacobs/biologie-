Lees de tekst mee

Hold your breath. Go on, just for a few seconds. Feel that urge to breathe again? That isn't your lungs running out of oxygen. It's your brain noticing something else entirely: carbon dioxide. Every cell in your body is breaking down glucose right now, and one of the waste products is carbon dioxide. It dissolves in your blood and makes it slightly more acidic. Sensors in your brainstem pick up that change within seconds. So here's a question for you. When you hold your breath, what makes you want to breathe again: too little oxygen, or too much carbon dioxide? Pause the video if you'd like to think about it. It's the carbon dioxide. Your body keeps an eye on the waste, not on the supply. Going a little deeper: some free divers breathe very fast before a dive. That lowers their carbon dioxide, so the warning signal comes late. And that can be dangerous.
