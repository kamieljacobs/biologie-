Every second, your body makes about two million new red blood cells. Each one is packed with haemoglobin, the protein that carries oxygen from your lungs to your mitochondria. But here's a question: why don't red blood cells have a nucleus? Think about it for a moment. Without a nucleus there's more room for haemoglobin, so each cell can carry more oxygen. Even the bacterium Escherichia coli has its own clever trick. Its DNA, deoxyribonucleic acid, can copy itself in about forty minutes, while the cell grows to just 0.002 millimetres long.
